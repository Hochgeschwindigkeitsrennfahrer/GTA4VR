# Physics grabbing (#018)

## Current replacement: full-pose loose-prop motor (2026-10-06)

The active source no longer creates an attachment proxy or hooks constraint creation for grabbing. Accepted native arm-contact spheres remain. Grabbing stores the original object's full rigid pose relative to the tracked wrist, then applies bounded native continuous force and force couples to follow that target through the contact solver. Foreign transforms, mass and ownership are not rewritten. Deliberate final-grip release can transfer bounded recent tracked linear velocity; safety releases retain native momentum. See the 2026-10-07 release section below.

Tracked targets are saved **before** IK/collision feedback. Previously the published rotation came from the solved wrist; using that as the next physics target could feed collision feedback into itself. Palm/fingertip targets now also rotate into the independently tracked frame. While holding, displayed wrist position and rotation come from the actual object and the captured grasp.

Acquisition uses CE `A535E0`'s stationary sphere-overlap path (`param9=1`) within configurable `HandGrabRadius` of the palm (default22cm, clamped5-35cm; restart to apply), testing eligible CObject instances individually and ranking by collision-surface proximity through four overlap-radius bisections. A 3m model-origin broadphase limits candidate work; this is a first-pass bound and may exclude unusually large offset-origin props. The original grasp offset is retained; there is no model-origin snap. Two hands each capture a grasp, produce one combined rigid target and share one bounded motor; releasing either leaves the other grasp active.

Linear/angular control uses implicit critically damped springs at 12Hz, capped tracked target velocity (5m/s, 12rad/s) and configured `HandGrabForce` per hand. Angular damping uses successive object poses rather than assuming native angular-getter units. Inertia is a uniform-box estimate from model dimensions, **not** GTA's exact inertia tensor. Native vector output copyback is now finalized even for void calls so `GET_MODEL_DIMENSIONS` reaches its output arguments. Divergence over the recovery distance, observed speed over 20m/s or 60rad/s, invalid handles and lifecycle loss release the grip instead of accumulating catch-up. These bounds are experimental, not accepted comfort/throw behavior.

The user accepts loose-prop wrist rotation, scenery blocking and two-handed furniture in the full-pose replacement. Whole-vehicle grabbing remains excluded after the preceding unstable attachment experiment. Native mission pickup coordination remains pending. Distributed opt-in defaults remain off; existing semantic Grab bindings and local strength settings are retained.

`hand-status` identifies `grabController: full-pose` and reports position error, angle error in radians, observed angular speed, applied torque, radius/inertia estimate, two-hand updates and unsafe releases. Math tests cover fixed grasp round-trips, world-axis rotation including 180 degrees, two-hand composition, invalid poses and timestep stability. Acquired props use a partly open finger curl with a relaxed thumb; release restores free-hand controls, and native weapon grips keep precedence.

### Native hinged car-door adapter (2026-10-06, runtime acceptance pending)

On a loose-prop miss, acquisition tests the passenger doors, bonnet and trunk of nearby vehicles. All valid lock states can acquire a panel grasp; only states0/1 may send hinge-opening/unlatch requests. Locked grasps follow the actual stationary panel and never unlock the car. Native fragment groups identify the door's compound collision-part range. A stationary palm overlap queries the vehicle root/door child and accepts only an actual returned hit+0x52 part in that range (or the exact independent door child). Radius bisection ranks accepted door contacts by proximity. Other car panels do not acquire a whole-car grab. Broken doors, unsupported/nonphysical records and invalid frames are rejected.

Each grasp captures wrist/palm placement relative to the door's actual world bone matrix. Palm displacement around its hinge axis becomes a clamped native opening fraction; motion along the hinge axis does not open it. `C2F5A0` in native `OPEN_CAR_DOOR` mode0x400 drives the existing capped 1DOF motor and retains native contacts and angle limits. While grabbed, door-local auto-latch bit16 and pending-close bit17 are canceled; final release restores the original auto-latch policy and requests a free hinge. Both hands retain the original policy for either release order. The vehicle lock is checked separately before acquisition and during holding. A locked grasp never clears latch flags; final release skips native hinge requests unless this grasp previously controlled the hinge. Release preserves an existing closed latch, and a native lock arriving mid-grab cancels our motor while retaining that latch. No car force, transform, velocity or lock writes are used. Displayed hands follow the actual door grasp; two hands can share one hinge target. Door-child contact is excluded from the owned hand shapes without disabling player contact with the vehicle root.

CE1.2.0.59 static/live mapping: vehicle+F88/F8C stores 0x34-byte records; IDs5/7/6/8 map to skeleton bones through model+CC. Record byte+4 selects the physics child, byte+5 the fragType+D4 descriptor, whose group maps collision-part indices through fragType+D0 group+10E/+10F. These three indices are distinct in observed cars. Bone matrices are world-space skeleton+14. Native `CONTROL_CAR_DOOR` hash194F76D4: BC5340 ->BC9820 ->C2F5A0(fraction,0x200). Native physical update C2E810 sets the hinge target, gains50/10 and force bounds +/-500 (250 for its slow-mode flag); these native caps are independent of `HandGrabForce`, not verified SI torque units. `GET_CAR_DOOR_LOCK_STATUS` reads vehicle+12D0 at BCA650. Findings were previewed, read back and saved in Ghidra under `Issue018CarDoorGrab`.

Diagnostics expose `doorAdapter`, `doorGrabbing`, `doorStarts`, `doorUpdates`, lock rejections/misses and actual/target fraction. Hinge math checks cover left/right opening, closing, vertical motion rejection and degenerate lever arms. The user accepts live grabbing, slamming and opening some closed doors, but reports other unlocked latched doors refusing to open and acquisition being too precise. The correction now uses OPEN mode0x400 instead of CONTROL mode0x200, because C2E8D7-C2E901 can re-latch a bit17 pending-close door under mode0x200. C2EEAB-C2EEF1 also shows the near-closed automatic/pending latch paths. Findings were saved as `Issue018DoorLatch` in Ghidra. Corrected-build reopening and locked-door rejection still need acceptance. Props and doors now share a configurable22cm acquisition radius; arm-contact shapes are unchanged. Bonnet/trunk indices4/5 (record IDs39/40) now use the same adapter, pending live acceptance. Sliding doors and detached doors remain unsupported.

## Historical initial loose-prop implementation

The following sections retain the investigation history. Proxy attachments and vehicle-root grabbing described below are superseded by the current replacement above.

`ExperimentalHandGrab=1` enables the experimental grab path on top of `ExperimentalHandPhysics=1`. Distributed defaults are off. SteamVR exposes **Grab with left hand** and **Grab with right hand** in On foot, suggested on each Wand's squeeze button. Holding grip searches nearby props every 50ms until one is acquired; holding maintains the grab, releasing drops it. Release the button after startup/tracking recovery before acquiring. The right hand cannot acquire a prop while it contains the native equipped object. Left-hand pistol support remains available; an acquired prop takes precedence over support posing.

For an existing custom SteamVR profile, assign both new Grab actions to their respective squeeze buttons in **On foot**, retaining the existing hand-close/support bindings. Suggested defaults do not establish that an older custom profile has adopted new actions. `tools/game-command.ps1 input-status` reports `grabActive` (bound/active this sync) and `grab` (currently held). Interpret them only while the session is FOCUSED, both Wands are tracked and gameplay is on foot. `hand-status` reports cumulative `gripSamples`, `grabAttempts`, `grabHits`, `grabStarts` and `grabRejected`: zero grip samples indicates routing/gating; attempts with no hits indicates reach/query; hits without starts indicates eligibility/attachment. Tracking recovery requires releasing grip before a fresh acquisition press.

The driving hand creates one additional invisible native rigid grip body while acquiring/holding a prop; a second hand on the same body adds support strength without creating another joint; release deletes that owned grip body. The ordinary ten contact nodes remain unchanged while not grabbing. `ATTACH_OBJECT_TO_OBJECT_PHYSICALLY` attaches this **owned proxy as child** to the original foreign prop, retaining the initial relative rotation. This avoids changing the foreign prop's attachment owner. Two-hand support shares the first hand's drive, avoiding conflicting rigid targets; releasing the driving hand currently releases both grips (handoff is not implemented). `HandGrabForce=900` caps the held-object linear motor per hand (25..3000 native units, restart to apply); accepted free-contact strength remains `HandPhysicsArmForce`. While holding, ordinary contact nodes share20% of the arm-contact budget. A bounded force couple controls orientation on the owned body. Props receive forces through the native constraint/contact solver, not manual prop impulses or per-frame position writes.

On release, only our proxy's attachment is removed. The intended throw behavior is retained native linear/angular momentum; no artificial throw boost. Owner changes, vehicle entry, stale tracking/focus, discontinuities and large target separation remove owned bodies and their native constraints. Object handles retain pool generation; foreign objects are never deleted by this module.

Acquisition uses the existing native sphere-volume query near the wrist/palm, excludes owned proxies/player, and resolves occupied CObject or CVehicle pool slots. Props require existing non-fixed physics; sleeping dynamic props may wake through the native dynamic setter. Vehicles use their existing physics body and native car attachment, without unlocking, detaching or teleporting the vehicle. There is no model whitelist or mass acquisition cutoff: capped motor strength determines whether an acquired body can actually be moved/lifted. Articulated car doors still need the fragment/hinge adapter; acquiring the car body does not implement pulling its door.

## Design correction after latest user test (2026-10-06)

The current attachment experiment has failed basic rotational grasp and two-hand acceptance. Improved furniture strength does not establish a sustainable grab system. Retain the accepted arm-contact bodies; replace the grabbing controller before more strength tuning. A disappeared vehicle is an unresolved failure, not evidence of a successful heavy-object grab.

Public references:

- Valve developer Kerry Davis describes Alyx's deliberately solid physical hands and world collision in [this interview](https://www.pcgamesn.com/half-life-alyx/valve-interview). This supports the interaction goal, not a claim about its exact solver/joint code.
- [Unity XR Grab Interactable](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.xr.interaction.toolkit@3.0/manual/xr-grab-interactable.html) documents collider-distance acquisition, captured attachment position/rotation, velocity-driven physical motion, and multiple-hand target transformers. Instantaneous transforms and kinematic movement have different collision/force tradeoffs.
- [Epic's Physics Handle](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/Engine/UPhysicsHandleComponent) exposes target position/rotation and separate angular/linear stiffness and damping. This is a public example, not a GTA-compatible dependency.

Proposed GTA design (not yet implemented or accepted):

1. Select a nearby physical surface with overlap/proximity ranking, independently of whether the hand happens to move across it. Capture a stable local position AND orientation for the grasp.
2. Give each held object one controller. Drive its complete target pose through verified native physics constraints/motors with bounded force/torque, mass/inertia-aware response and timestep handling. Let contacts resolve motion; visible hands follow the constrained grasp. Large/heavy objects may lag or resist, while light props should retain their orientation firmly.
3. Combine two hand anchors into one object target. The current second-hand force multiplier is not this implementation. Define release/handoff and degenerate-anchor behavior explicitly.
4. Handle doors as articulated fragments with native hinge/latch/lock state. Attaching to the vehicle root does not manipulate a door. Preserve native mission pickup recognition separately.
5. Gate progress on a mug rotating correctly through all axes and scenery contact, then two-hand chair control, then doors, then heavy vehicles. Diagnose full-pose error and unstable body motion before changing force limits again.

This is an implementation recommendation based on documented VR interaction patterns and observed failures. Exact Alyx mechanics are not established by these sources; native GTA joint suitability remains to be verified. No runtime changes were made for this reassessment.
